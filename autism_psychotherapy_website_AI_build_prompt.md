# Master AI Build Prompt --- Autism-Focused Psychotherapy Website

## Your Role

Act as a senior UX/UI designer, brand strategist, conversion-focused
copywriter, accessibility specialist, SEO strategist, and full-stack web
developer.

Design and build a polished, production-quality website for a private
psychology practice specializing in psychotherapy related to the autism
journey.

Do not create a generic therapist template. The finished website should
feel distinctive, sophisticated, calm, deeply human, and professionally
credible. It should communicate extensive clinical experience without
feeling institutional, cold, childish, or overly medical.

When information is not yet available, use clearly labeled placeholders
rather than inventing credentials, licenses, fees, locations,
affiliations, testimonials, or claims.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 1. Core Practice Positioning

The psychologist has:

-   A Doctor of Psychology (PsyD) degree from George Washington
    University.
-   More than 21 years of experience practicing as a licensed
    psychologist.
-   More than 15 years of direct clinical experience conducting autism
    assessment in early childhood.
-   A psychodynamic orientation.
-   A telehealth-focused psychotherapy practice.
-   A particular interest in the emotional, relational, developmental,
    and identity-related experiences surrounding autism diagnosis.

The central strategic idea is:

**15+ years understanding autism through assessment. More than two
decades understanding people through psychology. Now bringing those
perspectives together in psychotherapy.**

The practice is not simply about treating a list of autism symptoms. It
is about helping people understand what an autism diagnosis means in the
context of identity, relationships, family, development, and life
experience.

# 2. Primary Audiences

Design the website so each of these audiences immediately feels
included.

### Adults

Adults who:

-   Were recently diagnosed with autism.
-   Were diagnosed later in life.
-   Are beginning to understand themselves through an autistic lens.
-   Are reconsidering childhood or adult experiences after diagnosis.
-   Want to explore identity, masking, relationships, belonging, work,
    family, or self-understanding.

### Parents

Parents who:

-   Have a child who has received an autism diagnosis.
-   Are processing the emotional impact of diagnosis.
-   Want space for their own feelings rather than therapy solely focused
    on their child.
-   Are reconsidering expectations, parenting experiences, family
    relationships, or the future.

### Spouses and Partners

Partners who:

-   Are navigating an autism diagnosis within a relationship.
-   Want to better understand communication, intimacy, expectations,
    recurring patterns, or changing relationship dynamics.
-   Need a thoughtful space for their own experience.

### Siblings and Other Family Members

Family members who:

-   Are reconsidering family dynamics through the context of autism.
-   Carry complicated feelings, roles, expectations, responsibilities,
    or unresolved experiences.
-   Want to better understand themselves and their relationships.

# 5. Therapeutic Approach

The practice is psychodynamic.

Explain psychodynamic therapy in accessible language rather than
academic jargon.

Communicate that therapy may explore:

-   Current concerns
-   Earlier experiences
-   Relationships
-   Family dynamics
-   Attachment
-   Identity
-   Expectations
-   Repeating patterns
-   Communication
-   Emotional experiences
-   How people understand themselves
-   How people connect with others

A central idea:

Therapy is curious rather than prescriptive.

The psychologist should not sound like someone telling clients what
their diagnosis is supposed to mean. Instead, therapy provides space to
discover what it means to the individual.

Use language such as:

**My goal is not to tell you what your diagnosis should mean. It is to
help you understand what it means to you.**

# 8. Full About Page Copy Direction

Create an authoritative but personal About page using this content as
the foundation:

## About \[NAME\], PsyD

For more than 21 years, I have worked as a licensed psychologist, with
over 15 years of that experience devoted to working directly with
children and families in the assessment of autism in early childhood.

This experience has given me the opportunity to see firsthand that a
diagnosis is never simply a clinical label. It can become part of a much
larger personal and family story---one that can affect how people
understand themselves, their children, their relationships, and their
hopes for the future.

I earned my Doctor of Psychology degree from George Washington
University. My clinical perspective is grounded in psychodynamic
thinking and shaped by years of working closely with individuals and
families as they navigate complex emotional and relational experiences.

### Beyond the diagnosis

My psychotherapy practice grew from an interest in what happens after
the assessment.

For parents, receiving an autism diagnosis for a child can bring relief
and clarity, while also bringing uncertainty, worry, grief, or questions
about what the future may hold. Parents may find themselves
reconsidering expectations, family dynamics, and the ways they
understand their child.

For adults who discover that they are autistic later in life, diagnosis
can be equally transformative. Experiences from childhood and adulthood
may take on a different meaning. There may be recognition alongside
questions about identity, relationships, masking, belonging, and how to
move forward with a new understanding of oneself.

Autism can also affect the broader relationship system. Siblings,
spouses, partners, and other family members can have their own process
of understanding and adjustment.

### A psychodynamic approach

I believe these experiences deserve more than a checklist of symptoms or
a set of recommendations. They deserve space for reflection.

My approach is warm, curious, and psychodynamic. Together, we can
explore deeper patterns, relationships, expectations, and experiences
that influence how you see yourself and relate to the people around you.

There is no single way to experience an autism diagnosis. Relief,
recognition, uncertainty, frustration, grief, love, hope, and curiosity
may coexist.

My goal is not to tell you what your diagnosis should mean. It is to
help you understand what it means to you.

### Experience

After more than two decades as a psychologist, I continue to believe
that one of the most meaningful things therapy can offer is a place to
feel genuinely understood.

15+ years of direct experience in early-childhood autism assessment

**Practice**\

# 13. Accessibility

Accessibility is especially important for this audience.

Meet WCAG 2.2 AA wherever practical.

Requirements:

-   Semantic HTML.
-   Keyboard-accessible navigation.
-   Visible focus states.
-   Correct heading hierarchy.
-   Adequate color contrast.
-   Descriptive link labels.
-   Form labels that do not rely only on placeholders.
-   Useful validation messages.
-   Alt text strategy.
-   Reduced-motion support.
-   Responsive text sizing.
-   No flashing content.
-   Avoid unnecessary sensory overload.
-   Avoid overly dense pages.
-   Avoid auto-playing media.

Do not claim formal accessibility certification unless independently
verified.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 14. Neurodiversity-Respectful Language

Use identity-respecting, non-pathologizing language.

Prefer language such as:

-   autistic adult
-   autistic identity
-   autism diagnosis
-   understanding oneself through an autistic lens
-   individual experience
-   neurodiversity

Recognize that language preferences differ between individuals.

Avoid:

-   "suffering from autism"
-   "victim of autism"
-   "defeating autism"
-   "curing autism"
-   "normal people" versus autistic people
-   deficit-heavy copy
-   language implying autistic identity is inherently tragic

Do not make assumptions about support needs based solely on diagnosis.

# 15. Clinical and Ethical Guardrails

This is a psychologist's professional website. Treat accuracy and ethics
as critical.

Do not:

-   Guarantee clinical outcomes.
-   Claim to cure autism.
-   Invent board certifications.
-   Invent professional affiliations.
-   Invent licensure states.
-   Invent insurance participation.
-   Invent pricing.
-   Invent testimonials.
-   Create fake patient reviews.
-   Imply a therapeutic relationship is created by submitting a form.
-   Claim a contact form is secure/HIPAA-compliant without confirmation.
-   Diagnose website visitors.
-   Use manipulative scarcity language.

Clearly distinguish educational website content from professional advice
where appropriate.

Use placeholders for all unknown facts.
